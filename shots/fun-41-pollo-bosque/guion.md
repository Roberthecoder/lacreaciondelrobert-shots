# Shot Fungi Mati #41 · Esto no es pollo: el hongo que se come un árbol (*Laetiporus*)

- **Serie:** Shot Fungi Mati · **Logo:** biología (ADN) · **Badge:** #41 · rama `pedido300/fun`.
- **Formato:** N1 "Por dentro" (macro: un plato con algo que parece pollo deshebrado → el tronco con sus repisas naranjas → corte del tronco y pudrición café en cubitos → **al microscopio**: hifas de pared gruesa → el caso de 1987 → precauciones → el mismo plato del inicio, ya transformado: junto a él, el tronco hecho cubitos cafés).
- **Gancho (de los 7):** #1 "Esto no es X: es Y" sobre lo que se ve en pantalla: «Esto no es pollo: es un hongo que se está comiendo un árbol por dentro».
- **Duración:** voz 1000 caracteres / 189 palabras (≈ 58 s) + cierre 3.5 s → **61.5 s** en la base.
- **Voz:** pendiente de aprobación de Robert (~1000 créditos de ElevenLabs).
- **Tono:** curioso y útil (prevención). Nada invita a recolectar: no se enseña a identificarlo ni dónde buscarlo. No es consejo médico.

**Música:** cama grave continua, curiosa y algo tensa en el caso de la niña, más ligera al final; sin golpe marcado, debajo de la voz. Efectos en el mismo cuadro: tenedor y golpe del sello, crujido de las repisas al brotar, roce de las hifas y chasquidos de las cadenas de celulosa al romperse, crujidos de la madera al agrietarse y desmoronarse, "?" del re-gancho, chisporroteo de la sartén, zumbido del microscopio, tic de la tarjeta, tambaleo y "glitch" suave de la alucinación, tic-tac del cronómetro y campanita al amanecer, gruñido de estómago, golpe del sello "no lo recolectes", gota de colorante, crujido final de los cubitos.

Barras de escala: 5 cm (plato, sartén, crudo, zanahoria, cierre), 20 cm (repisas en el tronco), 10 cm (corte del tronco), 20 µm (hifas), 50 cm (la niña).

mecanismo de la pudrición se cuenta rápido; el dato extra (s 50–54) y el cierre sí caen en su lugar.

## Fuentes (cada dato se abrió en el texto completo el 29 oct 2026)

1. **[UF]** Benitez, B., Paez, C. A., Smith, M. E. y Smith, J. A. (2020). *Chicken of the Woods (Laetiporus sulphureus species complex)*. UF/IFAS Extension, EDIS PP358. https://doi.org/10.32473/EDIS-pp358-2020 (PDF: https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/PP358/pdf)
   > "All of the species of the "chicken of the woods" group cause brown rot in a variety of tree host species, and this brown rot can cause wood to lose its structural integrity and turn powdery."
   > "wood-decay fungi that cause brown rot within the heartwood of their tree hosts"
   > "cause brown rot decay of wood by selective degradation of cellulose and hemicellulose"
   > "most common in hardwood host trees (especially on species of Quercus [oaks] and Prunus spp. [cherry])"
   > "Fruiting bodies typically grow in clusters, and they often grow overlapping one another in layers, like roof shingles on a house"; "They usually do not have a stipe"; "vary in color from a bright yellow to orange"
   > "the mycelium (the microscopic filaments that constitute the body of the fungus)"
   > "because the fruiting bodies have a chewy texture and pleasant flavor that is similar to chicken when they are thoroughly cooked and eaten. Raw or undercooked Laetiporus mushrooms have been reported to cause gastrointestinal problems, and at least some individuals are allergic to "chicken of the woods""
   > "Members of the public should never consume wild mushrooms that they cannot positively identify as edible."
   > "Several Laetiporus species have been harvested to use as food colorants"
2. **[KUO]** Kuo, M. (2017, noviembre). *Laetiporus sulphureus*. MushroomExpert.Com. https://www.mushroomexpert.com/laetiporus_sulphureus.html
   > "Parasitic and saprobic on living and dead oaks (also sometimes on the wood of other hardwoods)"
   > "causing a reddish brown cubical heart rot, with thin areas of white mycelium visible in the cracks of the wood"
   > "Contextual hyphal system dimitic." "Contextual binding hyphae 4–14 µm wide; often branching; aseptate; smooth; walls 1–2 µm thick"
   > "Caps: 5–25 cm across"; "bright yellow to bright orange when fresh"
3. **[FUN]** Fungiflora (Ryvarden y col.), "Introduction to poroid fungi · Decay". https://www.fungiflora.no/intro-poroide-fungi-decay
   > "Brown rot fungi selectively remove cellulose and hemicellulose from wood."
   > "In advanced stages wood is reduced to a residue of amorphous, crumbly, brown cubical chunks composed largely of slightly modified lignin."
4. **[VAN]** Vanden Wymelenberg, A. et al. (2010). Comparative Transcriptome and Secretome Analysis of Wood Decay Fungi *Postia placenta* and *Phanerochaete chrysosporium*. *Appl. Environ. Microbiol.* 76(11): 3599–3610. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2876446/
   > "As decay progresses, brown rot fungi modify lignin extensively, but the products remain in situ as a polymeric residue."
5. **[APP]** Appleton, R. E., Jan, J. E. y Kroeger, P. D. (1988). *Laetiporus sulphureus* causing visual hallucinations and ataxia in a child. *CMAJ* 139(1): 48–49. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1267987/ (PMID 3383041; página escaneada leída)
   > "In August 1987 a 6-year-old girl was found to be clumsy and to be slurring her speech."
   > "One particularly frightening hallucination consisted of a "yellow and orange monster" that floated toward her."
   > "When seen at the local hospital the patient was afebrile and ataxic." / transferida al "British Columbia's Children's Hospital, Vancouver"
   > "The girl was completely recovered by the next morning, about 20 hours after the onset of symptoms."
   > "Her sister subsequently admitted seeing the child eat a tree-growing mushroom about 1 hour before the hallucinations started." "The mushroom was identified as L. sulphureus."
6. **[SUL]** Sułkowska-Ziaja, K., Muszyńska, B., Gawalska, A. y Sałaciak, K. (2018). *Laetiporus sulphureus* – chemical composition and medicinal value. *Acta Sci. Pol. Hortorum Cultus* 17(1): 87–96. https://doi.org/10.24326/asphc.2018.1.8
