# Shot Ambiental #53 · Este mar no está en silencio (el ruido de los barcos y el estrés de las ballenas)

- **Serie:** Shot Ambiental · **Logo:** física (átomo: el sonido bajo el agua) · **Badge:** #53 · rama `pedido300/amb`.
- **Formato:** N5 "El experimento" (el 11 de septiembre de 2001 como experimento accidental: menos barcos → menos ruido → menos hormonas de estrés), con un poco de N2 (los decibeles como medidor). Cierre que regresa al mar "en silencio" del inicio, ahora con los barcos lentos.
- **Gancho (de los 7):** #1 "Esto no es X: es Y" sobre lo que se ve: un mar tranquilo que parece en silencio → "Este mar no está en silencio: abajo hay un ruido que puede subirles el estrés a las ballenas."
- **No repite:** el shot de IA de cachalotes trata de su lenguaje; este trata del ruido de barcos y el estrés.
- **Duración:** voz 958 caracteres (11 líneas) ≈ 58 s con `velocidad` 1.1 + cierre 3.5 s → **61.5 s** en la base.
- **Cuentas:** 1,500 ÷ 340 = 4.4 → "más de cuatro veces más rápido" · 6 dB menos = 10^(−0.6) ≈ ¼ de la intensidad del sonido (solo para el dibujo del medidor, no se dice en la voz) · McDonald: 10–12 dB en ~38 años (1964–66 a 2003–04) = 2.5–3 dB por década → "casi tres decibeles cada década" · Barcos en Fundy: 9 barcos grandes (25 y 29 ago 2001) → 3 (12 y 13 sep 2001) → "muchos menos barcos".

## Fuentes (cada cifra se abrió en la fuente el 29 oct 2026)

1. **Rolland, R. M. et al. (2012). "Evidence that ship noise increases stress in right whales." *Proceedings of the Royal Society B* 279(1737): 2363–2368.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3350670 (también https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22319129/) — datos de 2001–2005, publicado en 2012.
   > "Baleen whales (Mysticeti) communicate using low-frequency acoustic signals. These long-wavelength sounds can be detected over hundreds of kilometres, potentially allowing contact over large distances."
   > "Low-frequency noise from large ships (20–200 Hz) overlaps acoustic signals used by baleen whales"
   > "the dominant source of human-generated low-frequency noise (20–200 Hz) is from the propellers and engines of commercial shipping vessels"
   > "reduced ship traffic in the Bay of Fundy, Canada, following the events of 11 September 2001, resulted in a 6 dB decrease in underwater noise with a significant reduction below 150 Hz. This noise reduction was associated with decreased baseline levels of stress-related faecal hormone metabolites (glucocorticoids) in North Atlantic right whales."
   > "This is the first evidence that exposure to low-frequency ship noise may be associated with chronic stress in whales"
   > "Ship traffic ... on the same dates in 2001 as the acoustic recordings, decreased from nine large ships on 25 and 29 August … to three ships on 12 and 13 September"
   > "The only year in which there was a significant decrease in fGCs after 11 September was 2001"
   > Limitación (dicha en la voz con "puede ligarse"): "this is a retrospective analysis based on a non-repeatable event, with all of the inherent limitations."
2. **McDonald, M. A., Hildebrand, J. A. & Wiggins, S. M. (2006). "Increases in deep ocean ambient noise in the Northeast Pacific west of San Nicolas Island, California." *J. Acoust. Soc. Am.* 120(2): 711–718.** https://www.cetus.ucsd.edu/docs/publications/McDonaldJASA2006.pdf — datos de 1964–1966 y 2003–2004.
   > "Ambient noise levels at 30–50 Hz were 10–12 dB higher (95% CI = 2.6 dB) in 2003–2004 than in 1964–1966, suggesting an average noise increase rate of 2.5–3 dB per decade."
   > "Increases in commercial shipping are believed to account for the observed low-frequency ambient noise increase."
3. **DOSITS (Discovery of Sound in the Sea, Universidad de Rhode Island), actividad "Stirring up Sound" (2024):** https://dosits.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Stirring_up_Sound-DOSITS_Activity.pdf
   > "Sound moves about 1500 meters per second in seawater (sound moves much more slowly in air, at about 340 meters per second). The speed of sound in seawater varies by a small amount (a few percent) from place to place, season-to-season, morning to evening, and with water depth."
4. **Programa ECHO (Vancouver Fraser Port Authority), presentación de Krista Trounce en el IMO Science Day, 21 mar 2019:** https://wwwcdn.imo.org/localresources/en/OurWork/Environment/Documents/LC_LP/SCIENCE%20DAY%202019%20PRESENTATIONS/2019-03-12%20-%20IMO%20Science%20Day%202019_ECHO%20Program%20presentation.pdf — datos de 2017.
   > "Voluntary vessel slowdown … When: Summers of 2017 (61 days) and 2018 (111 days) … Where: ~16 nautical miles through critical whale foraging habitat in Haro Strait"
   > "2017 vessel slowdown results … 60% reported participation … 2-11 dB reduction at source … 2.5 dB ambient noise reduction"
   > "Note: a 3 dB reduction is roughly equivalent to a 50% reduction in sound intensity. Source: JASCO Applied Sciences"
   - Ubicación: Green Marine (19 oct 2017), https://green-marine.org/stayinformed/news/echo-programs-vessel-slowdown-trial-in-haro-strait-is-a-success/ : "The nine-week trial – running August 7 to October 6, 2017 – had an overall reported vessel participation rate of 61%." El estrecho de Haro está entre la isla de Vancouver y las islas San Juan.

## Notas
- "Ruido grave del mar" (escena Decadas) = ruido ambiental de 30–50 Hz en un sitio al oeste de la isla San Nicolás; el aumento es de ese sitio (los autores sugieren que puede representar al Pacífico noreste, pero no se dice en la voz).
- "Hormonas del estrés" = metabolitos de glucocorticoides en heces (fGC). El estudio es retrospectivo y con muestras chicas después del 11-S: por eso la voz dice "puede ligarse" y no "causa".
- El mapa de la Bahía de Fundy, la isla de Vancouver y las ondas son esquemáticos.
