- **Serie:** Shot Fungi Mati · **Logo:** biología (ADN) · **Badge:** 116 · rama `claude/fun-115-119-plagas-cultivos`.
- **Formato:** N1 · POR DENTRO (con un poco de N5: el campo de pruebas de Uganda como escena). Viaje macro → micro → macro:
  arbusto de agracejo junto a un trigal → tallo con pústulas → al microscopio (esporas) → ciclo y campaña de 1918 → Uganda → el mismo

  hongo que puede dejarte sin pan.» (No se compara con la roya del café, fun-24.)

## Fuentes (abiertas el 5 nov 2026; la verificación independiente va en `verificacion.md`)

1. Leonard, K. J. y Szabo, L. J. (2005). Stem rust of small grains and grasses caused by *Puccinia graminis*. *Molecular Plant Pathology* 6(2): 99–111. doi:10.1111/j.1364-3703.2005.00273.x (resumen en PubMed 20565642)
   > "P. graminis is a typical heteroecious rust fungus with the full complement of five distinct spore stages that occur during asexual reproduction on its gramineous hosts and sexual reproduction that begins in the resting spore stage and culminates on the alternate host, barberry (Berberis spp.)."
   > "About 8-10 days after infection, a pustule several millimetres long and a few millimetres wide is formed by rupture of the host epidermis from pressure of a mass of brick-red urediniospores produced in the infection."
   > "With age, the infection ceases production of brick-red urediniospores and produces a layer of black teliospores in their place"
2. Peterson, P. D., Leonard, K. J., Miller, J. D., Laudon, R. J. y Sutton, T. B. (2005). Prevalence and distribution of common barberry, the alternate host of *Puccinia graminis*, in Minnesota. *Plant Disease* 89: 159–163. https://www.ars.usda.gov/SP2UserFiles/ad_hoc/36400500Publications/PDP/prev_barberry.pdf
   > "A federal and state program operated from 1918 until the 1980s to eradicate common barberry (Berberis vulgaris), the alternate host of Puccinia graminis, from the major areas of cereal production in the United States. Over 500 million bushes were destroyed nationally during the program"

   > "Reemergence of barberry may serve as a source of new wheat stem rust races in future epidemics."
3. Pretorius, Z. A., Singh, R. P., Wagoire, W. W. y Payne, T. S. (2000). Detection of virulence to wheat stem rust resistance gene Sr31 in *Puccinia graminis* f. sp. *tritici* in Uganda. *Plant Disease* 84(2): 203. doi:10.1094/PDIS.2000.84.2.203B (PubMed 30841334)
