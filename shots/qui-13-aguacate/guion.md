## Fuentes (cada dato se abrió en la fuente original el 29 oct 2026)

1. **Polifenol oxidasa (PPO), polifenoles + oxígeno → quinonas; solo ocurre cuando enzima, fenoles y oxígeno se juntan; están separados en la fruta entera; dos átomos de cobre** — Hamdan N., Lee C.H., Wong S.L., Fauzi C.E.N.C.A., et al. "Prevention of Enzymatic Browning by Natural Extracts and Genome-Editing: A Review on Recent Progress." *Molecules* 27(3):1101 (2022). PMID 35164369 · PMC8839884 · doi:10.3390/molecules27031101.
   > "In the presence of oxygen, enzymatic browning takes place when these enzymes catalyze the oxidation of phenols to quinones." · "This reaction, however, does not occur in fresh fruit and vegetable as their enzymes become separated from its substrate and phenolic compounds by cell compartments." · "The enzymatic browning reaction is only triggered when those enzymes, phenolic compounds and oxygen come in contact with one another." · "The two copper atoms in the PPO enzyme interact with the substrate and molecular oxygen resulting in the enzymatic browning process." · (lista de frutas con estas enzimas: "mushroom, apple, potato, pear, banana, peach and avocado")
2. **Las quinonas se encadenan en melaninas (pigmentos cafés); la PPO actúa donde las células se rompen** — Tilley A., McHenry M.P., McHenry J.A., Solah V., Bayliss K. "Enzymatic browning: The role of substrates in polyphenol oxidase mediated browning." *Curr Res Food Sci* 7:100623 (2023). PMID 37954915 · doi:10.1016/j.crfs.2023.100623.
   > "Once o-diphenolic compounds are oxidised into o-quinones, they interact with other compounds to form brown pigmented melanins." · "It is thought that subsequent non-enzymatic reactions result in these compounds polymerising into dark pigments called melanins." · "As PPO only interacts with phenolic compounds where cells are ruptured, chlorogenic acid oxidation would only be expected at the wounding site."
3. **Estudio: 1975, Israel, tres variedades; más PPO = se oscurece más rápido** — Kahn V. "Polyphenol oxidase activity and browning of three avocado varieties." *J Sci Food Agric* 26(9):1319–1324 (septiembre 1975). doi:10.1002/jsfa.2740260910. Afiliación (OpenAlex/Crossref): "Division of Food Technology, Agricultural Research Organisation, Volcani Center, Bet Dagan, Israel".
   > "A comparison between the in vitro total and specific activity of polyphenol oxidase of three avocado varieties, Fuerte, Horeshim and Lerman, showed clear differences that were correlated with the in vivo rate of browning of the corresponding freshly cut mature fruit; Fuerte had the highest values, followed by Horeshim and then Lerman." · "It was therefore concluded that the differences in the rate of browning of the three avocado varieties studied were directly related to the PPO activities as expressed in the crude enzyme fraction."
4. **El ácido (cítrico) baja el pH y frena la PPO** — Moon K.M., Kwon E.-B., Lee B., Kim C.Y. "Recent Trends in Controlling the Enzymatic Browning of Fruit and Vegetable Products." *Molecules* 25(12):2754 (2020). PMID 32549214 · PMC7355983 · doi:10.3390/molecules25122754.
   > "PPO with an optimal activity at pH 5–7 shows inhibition below pH 3." · "Acidifying agents, such as citric acid, ascorbic acid, and glutathione can inactivate PPO by lowering the pH."
5. **Limón y plástico pegado; el hueso solo protege lo que toca porque tapa el aire; "golf balls"** — MacKinnon E. "Can an Avocado Pit Really Keep Guacamole From Turning Brown?" *Live Science* (12 ene 2012). https://www.livescience.com/33660-guacamole-avocado-pit-prevent-brown.html
   > "the pits are really effective at preventing browning only on the part of the guacamole's surface they touch." · "The pit protects the guac simply because it shields a portion of the dip's surface from exposure to air. You'd be just as well off plopping a few hardboiled eggs or some golf balls or an iPhone into your guacamole." · "plastic wrap tamped down snugly to the surface of the dip, to limit as much oxygen exposure as possible." · "The citrus fruits' relatively high acidity, along with their natural antioxidants and high vitamin C content, helps handicap PPO-driven oxidation."
6. **No está echado a perder; el hueso cubre las células; al machacar se pone café más rápido** — Leviss D. "Why do avocados turn brown so quickly — and are they OK to eat at that point?" *Live Science* (5 oct 2025), con Matthew Fatino, asesor de cultivos subtropicales, University of California Cooperative Extension (UC ANR). https://www.livescience.com/chemistry/why-do-avocados-turn-brown-so-quickly-and-are-they-ok-to-eat-at-that-point
   > "When you cut open an avocado, you are exposing the enzyme in the avocado cells to oxygen in the air," · "This oxidation produces a pigmented compound called melanin, which is brown in avocados." · "A bit of browning doesn't mean you should throw the avocado away, and you can still eat it." · "It's because the pit is covering a lot of the cells," · "if you smash up an avocado, it'll brown way more quickly, because there is more surface area exposed to oxygen"

**No hay estudio publicado del hueso** (no se encontró ninguno revisado por pares); por eso la voz no menciona "un estudio" para el hueso: solo el mecanismo (tapa el aire), que se sigue de las fuentes 1 y 5–6.

## Notas
- El dibujo es esquemático (la enzima como pinza, las células, las "bolsitas", la cadena de melanina, el corte del tazón).
- "No está podrido" se refiere al color café: es oxidación por enzima, no microbios. En la descripción: si huele mal, sabe agrio o tiene moho, eso sí es echado a perder; la capa café puede saber amarga (Fatino: la grasa puede ponerse amarga), se puede quitar con cuchara.
- El limón no "apaga" la enzima en el guacamole: la frena (la PPO se inhibe del todo por debajo de pH 3; el guacamole con limón no llega a eso). Por eso la voz dice "trabaja más lento". El limón también aporta vitamina C (antioxidante), matiz para la descripción.
- "Melaninas" en frutas es el nombre genérico de esos pigmentos; no es la misma melanina de la piel humana (por eso la voz no lo compara).
- Variedades del estudio de 1975: Fuerte (más PPO, más rápido), Horeshim y Lerman (menos). Eran aguacates israelíes de esa época; el Hass no estaba en el estudio.
