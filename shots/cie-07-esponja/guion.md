## Fuentes (texto completo abierto el 28 oct 2026; detalle en `verificacion.md`)

1. **Cardinale, M., Kaiser, D., Lueders, T., Schnell, S. y Egert, M. (2017).** Microbiome analysis and confocal microscopy of used kitchen sponges reveal massive colonization by *Acinetobacter*, *Moraxella* and *Chryseobacterium* species. *Scientific Reports* 7: 5791. Universidad Justus Liebig de Giessen y Universidad de Furtwangen (Alemania). PMC5517580.
   > "Image analysis showed local densities of up to 5.4 * 10^10 cells per cm^3" (2017)
   > "Fourteen used kitchen sponges… were collected in autumn 2012 from private households in the greater area of Villingen-Schwenningen (Baden-Württemberg, Germany)"
   > "362 bacterial OTUs remained" · "concentrating in internal cavities and on sponge surfaces, where biofilm–like structures occurred"
   > "regularly sanitized ('specially cleaned') sponges did not show significantly reduced bacterial numbers" · "Moraxella osloensis, is also known for generating malodor in laundry… might be responsible for bad smelling kitchen sponges, too"
   > "Sanitation by boiling or microwave treatment has been shown to significantly reduce the bacterial load… resistant bacteria survive the sanitation process and rapidly re–colonize" · "replacement of kitchen sponges, for example, on a weekly basis"

## Notas
- Estudio pequeño (14 esponjas). "Hasta 54 mil millones" es la densidad máxima local, no el promedio.
- *Moraxella osloensis* va en cursiva en pantalla.
- El microscopio y la playera son esquemáticos.
