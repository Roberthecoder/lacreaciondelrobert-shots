## Fuentes (abiertas el 5 nov 2026; frases textuales)

1. Lu, J.-L., Crowther, T. W., Delgado-Baquerizo, M., Liu, W., Jiang, Y., Sun, H. y Wang, Z. (2026). Microbial necromass contribution to topsoil organic carbon storage of natural and agricultural ecosystems. *Earth System Science Data* 18: 3697–3710. https://doi.org/10.5194/essd-18-3697-2026 (texto completo, CC BY).
   > "we compiled data on fungal and bacterial necromass along with soil organic carbon (SOC) from the 0–20 cm soil layer across 486 study sites (145 agricultural and 341 natural ecosystems)"
   > "on average, FNC is two times more important than BNC in explaining SOC in both agricultural and natural ecosystems"
   > Tabla 1, "Agricultural ecosystem (N = 145)": FNC/SOC "34.39 ± 0.67" %; BNC/SOC "15.65 ± 0.33" %. (Bosque: 29.11 % y 13.48 %; pastizal: 26.75 % y 14.34 %.)
   > "the contributions of FNC to SOC were approximately twice those of BNC"
   > "Although the living soil microbial biomass typically constitutes only about 2 % of SOC (a ratio referred to as the microbial quotient; Anderson and Domsch, 1989; Liu et al., 2023), microbial necromass has been shown to contribute more than half and up to approximately 80 % of SOC"
   > "The cell walls of fungi primarily consist of chitin (a nitrogen-containing polysaccharide) and β-glucans, whereas bacterial cell walls are mainly composed of peptidoglycan"
   > "microbial processing transforms these inputs into microbial necromass that can persist over long turnover times"
   > Método: FNC se calcula con glucosamina (GluN) "correcting total GluN for its bacterial share using MurA"; "BNC = MurA × 45".
   > "the predominance of fungal necromass may be attributed to its more recalcitrant cell wall composition (e.g., chitin) and slower decomposition rate"
2. Liang, C., Amelung, W., Lehmann, J. y Kästner, M. (2019). We show that microbial necromass can make up more than half of soil organic carbon."
