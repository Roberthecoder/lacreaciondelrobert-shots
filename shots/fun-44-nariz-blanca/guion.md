**Música:** cama grave, fría y continua (eco de cueva), sin golpe marcado; efectos en el mismo cuadro: escarcha que cae, goteo de la cueva, crujido de las hifas entrando, «despertar» (aleteo) cada vez que sube el contador, mordida de la barra de grasa, tic de los calendarios, flash de la foto, golpes suaves de las barras que caen, latido suave en el mapa de México.

## Fuentes (cada dato se abrió en el texto completo el 29 oct 2026)

1. Blehert, D. S. et al. (2009). Bat white-nose syndrome: an emerging fungal pathogen? *Science* 323(5911): 227. doi:10.1126/science.1163874. https://science.umd.edu/faculty/wilkinson/SFT/Blehert09Science.pdf
   > "The first evidence of bat white-nose syndrome (WNS) was documented in a photograph taken at Howes Cave, 52 km west of Albany, New York, on 16 February 2006." (dato de 2006)
   > "named for the visually striking white fungal growth on muzzles, ears, and/or wing membranes of affected bats"
   > "Fungal hyphae replaced hair follicles and associated sebaceous and sweat glands, breaching the basement membrane and invading regional tissue. Hyphae also eroded the epidermis of ears and wings"
   > "Isolates were initially cultured at 3°C, grew optimally between 5°C and 10°C … Temperatures in WNS-affected hibernacula seasonally range between 2° and 14°C, permitting year-round growth … of the psychrophilic fungus." (2008)
   > "The distinctive curved conidia" (esporas curvas, para el dibujo)
2. Reeder, D. M. et al. (2012). Frequent arousal from hibernation linked to severity of infection and mortality in bats with white-nose syndrome. *PLOS ONE* 7(6): e38920. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3380050/
   > "about 80–90% of the energy (depot fat) used during hibernation is consumed during these periodic arousals from torpor"
   > "WNS-affected bats aroused to euthermic body temperatures more frequently than unaffected bats"
   > "Unaffected bats had a mean TBL of 16.32±6.65 days" · WNS-affected bats found dead: 7.93 days (TBL = días seguidos en sopor). Datos de los inviernos 2008–2009 a 2010–2011, cuevas de Vermont, Virginia Occidental, Pensilvania y Michigan.
   > torpor with "Tb close to ambient temperature (2° to 8° for bats)" (cuerpo casi tan frío como la cueva)
3. U.S. Geological Survey (2025). *U.S. Geological Survey Science Strategy To Address White-Nose Syndrome and Bat Health in 2025–2029* (Circular 1560). https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/cir1560/full
   > "Since its discovery in 2006, the fungal disease known as white-nose syndrome (WNS) has killed millions of bats."
   > "WNS has also been detected in 40 States and 9 Canadian Provinces."
4. Cheng, T. L. et al. (2021). The scope and severity of white-nose syndrome on hibernating bats in North America. *Conservation Biology* 35: 1586–1597. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8518069/
   > "Three species … (Myotis septentrionalis, Myotis lucifugus, and Perimyotis subflavus) declined by more than 90%" (conteos de invierno en más de 200 sitios con el hongo, datos 1995–2018)
   > "counts of bats have declined by more than 90% at monitored hibernacula within the decade since WNS emerged"
5. Wibbelt, G. et al. (2010). White-nose syndrome fungus (*Geomyces destructans*) in bats, Europe. *Emerging Infectious Diseases* 16(8). https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/eid/article/16/8/10-0002_article
   > deaths "were not observed at collection sites"; mass mortality "ha[s] never been documented" in European countries with the fungus (Alemania, Suiza, Hungría).
6. Drees, K. P. et al. (2017). Phylogenetics of a fungal invasion: origins and widespread dispersal of white-nose syndrome. *mBio* 8(6): e01941-17. https://www.usgs.gov/publications/phylogenetics-a-fungal-invasion-origins-and-widespread-dispersal-white-nose-syndrome
   > "Although the definitive source for introduction of the North American population has not been conclusively identified … our data support the origin of the North American invasion by P. destructans from Europe rather than Asia." (por eso la voz dice «apunta a»)
7. Medina-Cruz, G. E. et al. (2025). Presence of white-nose syndrome in bats from Southern Mexico. *PLOS ONE*. doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0318461. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12088370/
   > *P. destructans* hallado en 8 *Myotis velifer*, 1 *Pteronotus parnellii* y una carcasa de *Balantiopteryx plicata*, en una cueva de San Pedro de los Molinos, Oaxaca; muestras de 2018, 2020 y 2023; PCR (ITS) y secuenciación. Los autores reconocen que en murciélagos vivos es difícil descartar contaminación por esporas. (Por eso la voz dice «lo reportó», no «está confirmado en todo México».)
   - Contexto: en el invierno de 2019 otro equipo (LECVT) revisó nueve sitios de hibernación en México y todas las muestras salieron negativas (Wildlife Management Institute, *Outdoor News Bulletin*, junio 2020: "all were found negative for the presence of Pd"). No sale en la voz.
