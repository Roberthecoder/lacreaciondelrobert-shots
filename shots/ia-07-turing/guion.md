## Fuentes (cada cifra se abrió en la fuente original el 28 oct 2026)

1. **Artículo publicado (versión más nueva):** Jones, C. R. y Bergen, B. K. "Large language models pass a standard three-party Turing test". *PNAS* 123(21): e2524472123 (mayo de 2026). DOI 10.1073/pnas.2524472123 · texto completo en PubMed Central PMC13214042 (abierto con la API de Europe PMC).
   > "Participants had 5 min conversations simultaneously with another human participant and one of these systems before judging which conversational partner they thought was human. When prompted to adopt a humanlike persona, GPT-4.5 was judged to be the human 73% of the time: significantly more often than interrogators selected the real human participant."
   > "Without these prompts, however, the same models performed significantly worse (38% and 36%)… ELIZA and GPT-4o (23% and 21%, respectively)."
   > "GPT-4.5-NO-PERSONA and LLAMA-NO-PERSONA had overall win rates of 36% and 38% respectively"
   > "the average interrogator accuracy is also the average human witness win rate, and is the complement of the AI win rate" (preprint, sección 2.1) → humano real elegido 27 % de las veces frente a GPT-4.5 con personaje.
   > "specifically a young person who is introverted, knowledgeable about internet culture, and uses slang"
   > "ELIZA: a rules-based chatbot from the 1960s"
   > "After exclusions, we analyzed 1,023 games with a median length of 8 messages across 4.2 min."
   > "Interrogators were most likely to be accurate when they said something strange… Asking about the weather or human experiences tended to be the least effective strategies."
   > "Models with this ability to robustly deceive (and masquerade as) people could be used for social engineering or to spread misinformation"
   > 15 minutos: "GPT-5 was judged to be human marginally more often than chance (59%)"; "confirming that both models pass the Turing test with the extended time limit."
   > Afiliación: "Department of Cognitive Science, University of California San Diego".
2. **Preprint (2025):** arXiv:2503.23674 "Large Language Models Pass the Turing Test" (31 mar 2025): "75 years ago, Alan Turing (1950) proposed the imitation game… If the interrogator cannot reliably identify the human, the machine is said to have passed".
