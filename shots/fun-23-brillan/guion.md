## Fuentes (abiertas en el texto completo el 29 oct 2026)

1. Kotlobay, A. A. et al. (2018). Genetically encodable bioluminescent system from fungi. *PNAS* 115(50): 12728–12732. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6294908/
   > "the biosynthetic cycle of the fungal luciferin from caffeic acid, a simple and widespread metabolite"
   > Fig. 1: ácido cafeico → hispidina (hispidin synthase) → 3-hidroxihispidina, "fungal luciferin" (H3H); "Oxyluciferin can be recycled to caffeic acid by caffeylpyruvate hydrolase (CPH)"
   > "emitted green light with a maximum at 520 nm"
   > "Approximately 100 fungal species from the order Agaricales emit light utilizing the same biochemical reaction."
2. Oliveira, A. G., Stevani, C. V., Waldenmaier, H. E., Viviani, V., Emerson, J. M., Loros, J. J. y Dunlap, J. C. (2015). Circadian Control Sheds Light on Fungal Bioluminescence. *Current Biology* 25(7): 964–968. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4382382/
   > Campo: Mata dos Cocais, Fazenda Cana Brava, Altos, Piauí, Brasil, marzo de 2013. Trampas: "mushrooms" de acrílico con LED verde y pegamento sin olor; controles con LED apagado.
   > 5 noches, 19 trampas de cada tipo: 42 insectos en las encendidas contra 12 en las apagadas (moscas y escarabajos significativos; chinches y avispas/hormigas al límite).
   > Micelio de *N. gardneri*: "oscillates in a circadian rhythm of ca. 22h at 25°C"; luz verde ≈ 530 nm; "only 71 species" conocidas en 2015.
   > Hipótesis de los autores: la luz atrae insectos que podrían dispersar las esporas.
3. Ke, H.-M. et al. (2020). Mycena genomes resolve the evolution of fungal bioluminescence. *PNAS* 117(49): 31267–31277. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7733832/
   > Cluster de la luciferasa "estimated to originate around 160 million years ago during the late Jurassic"; "At least 68 of the 81 known bioluminescent fungi belong to Mycena"; tres linajes (*Armillaria*, micenoides, *Omphalotus*).
4. Blanchette, R. A. (2024). Bioluminescent fungi. *Fungi* 17(1). https://www.fungimag.com/v17i1/V17I1-Blanchette.pdf
   > "light emitted from wood decayed by some fungi" ("foxfire"); Armillaria "produce white mycelial fans under the bark that are bioluminescent and others such as Panellus in temperate forests and Neonothopanus and Mycena in the tropics produce fruiting bodies that glow in the dark"
5. Cortés-Pérez, A. et al. (2019). New species and records of bioluminescent Mycena from Mexico. *Mycologia* 111(2). doi:10.1080/00275514.2018.1554172 · https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/65df441963b8185d9cabd5e5
   > "Seven species of bioluminescent fungi are recorded from the cloud forests in Mexico. Six represent new species belonging to the genus Mycena"

Notas para la verificación: "unas cien especies" sigue a Kotlobay 2018 (Oliveira 2015 contaba 71; Ke 2020, 81). Los 160 millones de años son una estimación del reloj molecular. Lo de los insectos que llevan esporas es la hipótesis de Oliveira et al.
