## Fuentes (abiertas el 28 oct 2026; detalle en `verificacion.md`)

1. **Blakemore, S.-J., Frith, C. D. y Wolpert, D. M. (1999).** Spatio-temporal prediction modulates the perception of self-produced stimuli. *J. Cogn. Neurosci.* 11(5): 551–559. University College London. PMID 10511643.
   > "subjects moved the arm of a robot with their left hand to produce the tactile stimulus on their right hand via a second robot… consistently rated self-produced tactile stimuli as less tickly, intense, and pleasant… The tickliness rating increased significantly with increasing delay and trajectory perturbation."
2. **Blakemore, S.-J., Wolpert, D. M. y Frith, C. D. (1998).** Central cancellation of self-produced tickle sensation. *Nature Neuroscience* 1: 635–640. PMID 10196573.
   > "This difference suggests that the cerebellum is involved in predicting the specific sensory consequences of movements, providing the signal that is used to cancel the sensory response to self-generated stimulation."
3. **Blakemore, S.-J., Wolpert, D. y Frith, C. (2000).** Why can't you tickle yourself? *NeuroReport* 11(11): R11–R16. PMID 10943682.
   > "It is well known that you cannot tickle yourself… A forward model predicts the sensory consequences of a movement based on the motor command."
4. **Ishiyama, S. y Brecht, M. (2016).** Neural correlates of ticklishness in the rat somatosensory cortex. *Science* 354: 757–760. PMID 27846607.
   > "Rats emit ultrasonic vocalizations in response to tickling by humans."
5. **Ishiyama, S., Kaufmann, L. V. y Brecht, M. (2019).** Behavioral and cortical correlates of self-suppression, anticipation, and ambivalence in rat tickling. *Current Biology* 29: 3153–3164. PMID 31564493.
   > "Self-touch induced wide-ranging inhibition and vocalization suppression."
