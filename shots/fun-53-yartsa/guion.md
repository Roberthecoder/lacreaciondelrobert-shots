# Shot Fungi Mati #53 · Esa ramita es una oruga: el hongo-oruga del Tíbet

## Fuentes (abiertas el 29 oct 2026)

1. Hopping, K. A., Chignell, S. M. y Lambin, E. F. (2018). The demise of caterpillar fungus in the Himalayan region due to climate change and overharvesting. *PNAS* 115(45): 11489–11494. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6233077/
   > "By 2017, high-quality pieces sold for more than 140,000 USD per kilogram in Beijing, more than three times the price of gold"
   > "harvesting has become a primary source of income for hundreds of thousands of collectors"
   > "an entomopathogenic fungus that parasitizes over 50 species of Thitarodes (Hepialidae) moth larvae in the Himalayan region"
   > "Caterpillar fungus emerges after snowmelt"
   > "dig infected caterpillars out of the ground with the fungus still attached, typically before it begins producing spores"
   > "caterpillar fungus production has decreased due to habitat degradation, climate change, and especially overexploitation"
   > "harvesters increasingly attribute declining production to overexploitation, while models indicate that climate warming is also contributing to this decline"
   > Métodos: el estudio abarca "the Tibetan Plateau in China and Himalayan regions of Bhutan, India, and Nepal"; la Fig. 2 reúne reportes de más de 816 entrevistados sobre cambios en la producción; "the majority of respondents observed that caterpillar fungus production has decreased". Afiliación: Hopping y Lambin, Stanford University (Chignell, Colorado State).
2. Guo, L.-X. et al. (2015). Morphological Observations and Fatty Acid Composition of Indoor-Cultivated *Cordyceps sinensis* at a High-Altitude Laboratory on Sejila Mountain, Tibet. *PLoS ONE* 10(5): e0126095. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4418754/

   > "The host larva gradually becomes hard and turns into a caterpillar-shaped sclerotium."
   > the fungus "ruptures the larval cuticle, generally at the head, and its stroma emerges from the topmost soil layer in late spring."
3. UICN, Lista Roja: *Ophiocordyceps sinensis*, evaluación del 31 oct 2019 (Vulnerable, A2bcd+3bcd+4bcd). Ficha 58514773 (consultada en la copia https://redlist.info/iucn/species_view/504340; la página oficial bloquea la lectura automática).

4. Nombre "yartsa gunbu" (tibetano) — Winkler, D. (2008). Yartsa Gunbu (*Cordyceps sinensis*) and the Fungal Commodification of Tibet's Rural Economy. *Economic Botany* 62: 291–305. https://doi.org/10.1007/s12231-008-9038-3 (el título usa el nombre; el texto completo es de pago).
