# Shot Fungi Mati #18 · El cacahuate arrugado del fondo de la bolsa: aflatoxinas

- **Serie:** Shot Fungi Mati · **Logo:** biología (ADN) · **Badge:** #18 · rama `pedido300/fun`.

## Fuentes (cada dato se abrió en el texto completo el 29 oct 2026)

1. CDC (2004). Outbreak of Aflatoxin Poisoning — Eastern and Central Provinces, Kenya, January–July 2004. *MMWR* 53(34): 790–793. https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/mm5334a4.htm
   > "As of July 20, a total of 317 cases had been reported, with 125 deaths (CFR = 39%)."
   > "The 2004 outbreak resulted from widespread aflatoxin contamination of locally grown maize, which occurred during storage of the maize under damp conditions."
   > (nota editorial) la cosecha fue en febrero, "during peak rains" (dato 2004).
   > "15 had >20 ppb aflatoxin B1 (range: 20--8,000 ppb)."
   > "aflatoxin concentrations are limited to 20 parts per billion (ppb), a level also adopted by Kenyan authorities."
   > Definición de caso: ictericia aguda ("acute jaundice") = piel amarilla.
   > Prevención: "maize is harvested correctly, dried completely, and stored properly."
2. OMS (2 oct 2023). Mycotoxins (hoja informativa). https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/mycotoxins
   > "produced by certain moulds (Aspergillus flavus and Aspergillus parasiticus)"
   > Alimentos: "cereals (corn…)", "oilseeds (… peanut …)", "spices (chili peppers …)", "tree nuts (pistachio, almond, walnut …)" — "which are all regularly contaminated with aflatoxins".
   > "inspect whole grains … and nuts for evidence of mould, and discard any that look mouldy, discoloured, or shrivelled"
   > "Mould usually does not grow in properly dried and stored foods"; "make sure that foods are stored properly – kept free of insects, dry, and not too warm".
   > "can be life threatening, usually through damage to the liver"; "There is also evidence that they can cause liver cancer in humans."
3. Singapore Food Agency (28 abr 2022). Aflatoxins and food safety. https://www.sfa.gov.sg/food-information/risk-at-a-glance/aflatoxins-and-food-safety
   > aflatoxins "cannot be easily identified because they are odourless, tasteless and colourless."
   > "Once produced, aflatoxins are chemically stable in food and cannot be easily eliminated from food … even at high temperatures encountered in food processing, such as roasting, baking, and even frying."
   > "These moulds are prevalent in tropical regions where temperatures and humidity are high."
4. IARC (1993). Monografía vol. 56, Aflatoxins (resumen y evaluación). https://inchem.org/documents/iarc/vol56/09-afl.html
   > "Naturally occurring aflatoxins are carcinogenic to humans (Group 1)."; "There is sufficient evidence in humans for the carcinogenicity of aflatoxin B1."
   > "Humans metabolize aflatoxin B1 to an 8,9-epoxide, forming DNA and albumin adducts"; "aflatoxin B1-N7-guanine".
   > Hongos comunes en regiones de "hot, humid climates". (Reafirmado en el vol. 100F, 2012.)
5. National Cancer Institute (EUA). Aflatoxins. https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/risk/substances/aflatoxins
   > "Exposure to aflatoxins is associated with an increased risk of liver cancer."
6. Pickova, D., Ostry, V., Toman, J. y Malir, F. (2021). Aflatoxins: History, Significant Milestones, Recent Data on Their Toxicity and Ways to Mitigation. *Toxins* 13(6): 399. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8227755/
   > "A total of 100,000 turkey poults died by hitherto unknown turkey "X" disease in England."
   > "The disease was associated with Brazilian groundnut meal affected by Aspergillus flavus."
   > "In the late 1950s and early 1960s, a new so far unknown turkey disease" … "identified in England" (de ahí "hacia 1960").
   > "temperatures up to 100 °C used for common food preparation have little effect on AFs"
