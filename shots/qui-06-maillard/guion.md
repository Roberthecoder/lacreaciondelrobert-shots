## Fuentes (cada dato se abrió en la fuente original el 28 oct 2026)

1. **Mecanismo, pirazinas, melanoidinas, temperatura y agua, Maillard en 1912, acrilamida e IARC, Maillard en frío** — El Hosry L. et al. "Maillard Reaction: Mechanism, Influencing Parameters, Advantages, Disadvantages, and Food Industrial Applications: A Review." *Foods* (2025). PMID 40509409 · PMC12154226.
   > "The Maillard reaction is a chemical reaction between amino acid residues and reducing sugars that leads to the formation of melanoidins, contributing to the distinct flavor and brown color of foods" · "pyrazines that are responsible for the roasted, nutty flavors" · "melanoidins, which are high molecular weight, brown-colored compounds" · "Reaction acceleration is generally observed with increasing temperature … reaction rate decreases under … extreme water activity values (a w < 0.4 or >0.9)" · "In 1912, Louis Camille Maillard, a French chemist, discovered a browning phenomenon when he heated a solution containing a sugar and an amino acid." · "acrylamide … classified as … a probable human carcinogen by the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC)" · "Maillard reaction activity at −18 °C over storage periods ranging from 30 to 120 days"
2. **2002, Inglaterra: acrilamida por Maillard, con asparagina de papa y cereales** — Mottram D.S., Wedzicha B.L., Dodson A.T. "Acrylamide is formed in the Maillard reaction." *Nature* 419:448–449 (2002). PMID 12368844.
   > "acrylamide can be generated from food components during heat treatment as a result of the Maillard reaction between amino acids and reducing sugars" · "asparagine, a major amino acid in potatoes and cereals, is a crucial participant" · "School of Food Biosciences, The University of Reading"
