## Fuentes (cada dato se abrió en la fuente original el 29 oct 2026)

1. **El estudio de 1920: anillo verde-negro = sulfuro ferroso en la superficie de la yema; el H₂S viene de la clara; agua fría; 15 vs 30 minutos; 7 horas y ~1 mm; la membrana deja pasar el H₂S** — Tinkler C.K., Soar M.C. "The Formation of Ferrous Sulphide in Eggs during Cooking." *Biochemical Journal* 14(2):114–119 (1920). PMID 16742889 · PMC1258902 · DOI 10.1042/bj0140114. Laboratorio: "From the Chemical Laboratory, Household and Social Science Department, King's College for Women." (Se leyeron las 6 páginas escaneadas en PMC.)
   > (p. 114) "on prolonged cooking of an egg in its shell (e.g. when it is immersed in boiling water for 15 minutes or longer) a greenish black coloration is produced on the surface of the yolk."
   > (p. 114) "It appeared probable at the outset of the investigation that the greenish black coloration was due to ferrous sulphide in a fine state of division, and the various experiments which have been made seem to confirm this view." · "It is well known that egg white on prolonged heating evolves considerable quantities of hydrogen sulphide and that the yolk of egg contains iron"
   > (p. 115) "the latter is only produced as a thin layer on the surface of the yolk."
   > (p. 116) "It must, however, be permeable to hydrogen sulphide." (la membrana de la yema)
   > (p. 116) "Two eggs were heated in boiling water for 15 minutes and two for 30 minutes. In both cases one of the eggs was cooled down quickly by removing the shell and placing in a stream of running water, while the other was allowed to cool with the water in which it had been heated. In the case of the eggs heated for 15 minutes practically no green colour was obtained on the yolk of the egg which was cooled quickly, whereas a slight green colour was apparent on the other. In the case of the eggs heated for 30 minutes, very little difference was apparent in the amount of green colour developed, whether the egg was cooled quickly or allowed to cool with the water; but a much more intense coloration was produced round the yolks of these eggs than round the yolks of those heated only for 15 minutes."
   > (p. 116) "on cooling the egg quickly, the preliminary decomposition necessary for the formation of ferrous sulphide is checked."
   > (p. 117) "the rapid cooling on the outside of the egg will lead to the diffusion of the hydrogen sulphide from the white away from the yolk, and in this way also diminish the amount of ferrous sulphide formed."
   > (p. 117) "two eggs were heated in boiling water, one for two hours, when still only a thin film of green round the yolk was apparent, and the other for seven hours. In the latter case the colour of the white after cooking was buff and the green layer was thicker than in the previous case, especially on one side where its thickness was about one millimetre."
   > (p. 119, Summary) "the hydrogen sulphide necessary for the formation of the substance being produced by the decomposition of a sulphur compound of the egg white."
2. **Londres (Kensington)** — Queen Elizabeth College Association, "College history" (qeca.org.uk/college-history). El Household and Social Science Department del King's College for Women estaba en Kensington, Londres:
   > "On a new site at Campden Hill, Kensington (the Blundell Hall estate)" · "buildings for the Household and Social Science Department … were begun in 1914 and went into use in 1915"
3. **Azufre de la clara + hierro de la yema = sulfuro ferroso; por cocer de más; no hace daño; enfriar rápido** — Henneman A. (revisado y actualizado 2024). "How to Avoid a Green Ring on Hard-Boiled Egg Yolks." University of Nebraska–Lincoln Extension, food.unl.edu/how-avoid-green-ring-hard-boiled-egg-yolks/
   > "The ring is caused by a chemical reaction involving sulfur (from the egg white) and iron (from the egg yolk), which naturally react to form ferrous sulfide at the surface of the yolk. The reaction is usually caused by overcooking, but can also be caused by a high amount of iron in the cooking water." · "It's unattractive, but not harmful." · "Eliminate the ring by avoiding overcooking and by cooling the eggs quickly after cooking."
4. **Inofensivo; American Egg Board: agua caliente, no hirviendo, y enfriar de inmediato** — Haley-Hadley C. "How to Make Perfect Boiled Eggs Without the Green Ring." University of Arkansas Division of Agriculture, Cooperative Extension (Miller County), uaex.uada.edu (sin fecha).
   > "The green ring is harmless and occurs when eggs have been cooked for too long or at too high a temperature." · "The American Egg Board suggests that you cook eggs in hot, not boiling, water, and then cool immediately to minimize the green ring."
5. **Sulfuro de hidrógeno = gas con olor a huevo podrido** — ATSDR/CDC. "ToxFAQs for Hydrogen Sulfide" (página revisada el 28 oct 2024). wwwn.cdc.gov/TSP/ToxFAQs/ToxFAQsDetails.aspx?faqid=388&toxid=67
   > "Hydrogen sulfide is a flammable, colorless gas that smells like rotten eggs."
6. **"Proteínas con azufre" de la clara (el H₂S sale de sus proteínas)** — Takahashi M., Handa A., Yamaguchi Y., Kodama R., Chiba K. "Anodic Oxidative Modification of Egg White for Heat Treatment." *J Agric Food Chem* 64(34):6503–7 (2016). PMID 27518910 · DOI 10.1021/acs.jafc.6b02785.
   > "The method involves electron transfer from thiol groups of egg white protein to form disulfide bonds. The oxidized egg white produced less hydrogen sulfide during heat treatment"
   Y Tinkler y Soar 1920: "a sulphur compound of the egg white" (p. 119) · "the yolk of egg contains iron, probably in combination with proteins" (p. 114).

## Notas
- El dibujo es esquemático (moléculas, granos de FeS, la membrana y las capas no están a escala).
- "Echado a perder": el anillo no indica que el huevo esté descompuesto; sale por cocción larga (fuentes 3 y 4). Ojo: un huevo crudo con mal olor sí puede estar echado a perder; eso no se dice en la voz.
- La UNL agrega que también puede salir por mucho hierro en el agua de cocción: va en la descripción, no en la voz.
- En 1920 los autores también vieron que la clara empieza a soltar H₂S tras ~3.5 minutos en agua hirviendo ("after about three and a half minutes", p. 118) y que la yema se vuelve alcalina al calentarse (p. 118–119): matices para la descripción.
- "Agua fría ya casi no ayudó" a los 30 min = "very little difference was apparent … whether the egg was cooled quickly or allowed to cool with the water".
- La American Egg Board se cita a través de la extensión de Arkansas (no se pudo abrir su página directa: 404); el consejo práctico de la voz ("no lo cuezas de más y enfríalo rápido") está textual en UNL y en Tinkler y Soar.
- Baker et al. 1967 (*Poultry Science* 46(3):664–672, DOI 10.3382/ps.0460664, "Factors Affecting the Discoloration of Hard-Cooked Egg Yolks") no se pudo abrir; no se usa.
