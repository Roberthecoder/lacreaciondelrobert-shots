## Fuentes (texto completo abierto el 28 oct 2026; detalle en `verificacion.md`)

1. **Sender, R., Fuchs, S. y Milo, R. (2016).** Revised estimates for the number of human and bacteria cells in the body. *PLoS Biology* 14(8): e1002533. Weizmann Institute of Science. PMC4991899.
   > "We estimate the total number of bacteria in the 70 kg 'reference man' to be 3.8·10^13… revise past estimates to 3.0·10^13 human cells… their total mass is about 0.2 kg."
   > "The primary source for the often cited value of ~10^14 bacteria in the body dates back to the 1970s and only consists of a sentence-long 'derivation,' which assumes the volume of the alimentary tract to be 1 liter"
   > "Values for a reference adult man averaged 0.4 L" · "each defecation event reduces the content by a quarter to a third" · "The cell count is dominated by red blood cells (84%)" · "B/H = 1.3… about 1:1"
2. **Sender, R., Fuchs, S. y Milo, R. (2016).** Are we really vastly outnumbered? Revisiting the ratio of bacterial to host cells in humans. *Cell* 164(3): 337–340. PMID 26824647.
   > "bacteria outnumber human cells by a ratio of at least 10:1. Revisiting the question, we find that the ratio is much closer to 1:1."

## Notas
- Son estimaciones para un hombre de referencia de 70 kg (incertidumbre ±25 % en 1.3, y varía entre personas).
- Si solo se cuentan células con núcleo, la proporción vuelve a ~10:1; el artículo cuenta los glóbulos rojos como células.
- "Billones" en español de México = millones de millones (10¹²).
