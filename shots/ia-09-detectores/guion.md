## Fuentes (cada cifra se abrió en la fuente original el 28 oct 2026)

1. Liang, W., Yuksekgonul, M., Mao, Y., Wu, E. y Zou, J. "GPT detectors are biased against non-native English writers". *Patterns* 4(7): 100779 (julio de 2023). DOI 10.1016/j.patter.2023.100779 · PMC10382961 (texto completo abierto con la API de Europe PMC). Universidad de Stanford.
   > "we evaluated the performance of seven widely used GPT detectors on 91 TOEFL (Test of English as a Foreign Language) essays from a Chinese forum and 88 US eighth-grade essays from the Hewlett Foundation's ASAP dataset. While the detectors accurately classified the US student essays, they incorrectly labeled more than half of the TOEFL essays as "AI-generated" (average false-positive rate: 61.3%). All detectors unanimously identified 19.8% of the human-written TOEFL essays as AI authored, and at least one detector flagged 97.8% of TOEFL essays as AI generated."
   > "Most GPT detectors use text perplexity to detect AI-generated text, which might inadvertently penalize non-native writers who use a more limited range of linguistic expressions."
   > "We used ChatGPT to enhance the vocabulary of TOEFL essays… the average false-positive rate dropping by 49.7% (from 61.3% to 11.6%)."
   > "upon prompting ChatGPT to self-edit its text with more literary language… detection rates plummeted to near zero"
   > "we strongly caution against the use of GPT detectors in evaluative or educational settings, particularly when assessing the work of non-native English speakers."
   > Figura 1: "near-perfect accuracy for US eighth-grade essays".

## Notas

- "Estudiantes de secundaria" = ensayos de octavo grado de EE. UU. (en México, segundo de secundaria).
