DOI 10.1145/3630106.3658996 · arXiv:2402.08021v2 (PDF completo leído). Afiliaciones: Cornell University (Koenecke, Choi), University of Washington (Mei), New York University (Schellmann), University of Virginia (Sloane).
   > Resumen: "While many of Whisper's transcriptions were highly accurate, we find that roughly 1% of audio transcriptions contained entire hallucinated phrases or sentences which did not exist in any form in the underlying audio. We thematically analyze the Whisper-hallucinated content, finding that 38% of hallucinations include explicit harms such as perpetuating violence, making up inaccurate associations, or implying false authority."
   > "We find that hallucinations disproportionately occur for individuals who speak with longer shares of non-vocal durations—a common symptom of aphasia."
   > "running 13,140 audio segments through the default Whisper API" · "we find that 1.4% of audio segments yielded a hallucination sequence in Whisper transcriptions based on experiments conducted in April and May 2023."
   > Ejemplo: "the audio segment whose actual audio contains only the words: "pick the bread and peanut butter." Instead, the April 2023 Whisper run yields the transcription "Take the bread and add butter. In a large mixing bowl, combine the softened butter.""
   > "among the 312 hallucinated transcriptions, 19% include harms perpetuating violence, 13% include harms of inaccurate associations, and 8% include harms of false authority. Across all hallucinated transcriptions, 38% contain at least one of these types of harms."
   > Categorías: "references to (a) made up names and/or locations, (b) made up human relationships, or (c) made up health statuses" · "False Authority: … (b) thanking viewers or specific groups" · ejemplo en Tabla 1: "Thanks for watching".
   > "Specifically, 1.7% aphasia audio segments yielded hallucinations, versus only 1.2% of control group audio segments"
   > Hipótesis: "longer pauses in spoken speech (thereby, with longer periods of background noise in the audio file) could result in more hallucinations due to Whisper being seeded by noise rather than speech." · "perhaps implying that Whisper's over-reliance on OpenAI's language modeling advancements is what leads to hallucinations." · "Whisper model could involve similar technology to hallucination-prone language model ChatGPT"
   > "we identified exactly 0 comparable hallucination concerns (as defined above) from Google's products out of the 187 identified audio segments. We similarly identified exactly 0 comparable hallucination concerns among the same 187 audio segments from Amazon, Microsoft, AssemblyAI, and RevAI speech-to-text services (tested in January 2024)."

## Notas
- "Toma el pan y la crema de cacahuate" / "en un tazón grande, mezcla la mantequilla" = traducción del ejemplo real del paper ("pick the bread and peanut butter" → "…In a large mixing bowl, combine the softened butter."). Se dice "crema de cacahuate" (México) por *peanut butter*.
- "Cerca de una de cada cien" = "roughly 1%" del resumen (1.4 % en los resultados, abril–mayo 2023).
- "Casi cuatro de cada diez" = 38 %. "Enfermedades falsas" = *made up health statuses*; "«gracias por ver»" = *thanking viewers* / "Thanks for watching" (falsa autoridad).
- "Pasó más con personas con afasia" = 1.7 % contra 1.2 % (p = 0.019). La causa (pausas/silencio) y el papel del modelo de lenguaje son **hipótesis de las autoras** y así se dice.
- "Esos audios problema" = los 187 fragmentos donde Whisper inventó; Google se probó en abril y diciembre de 2023, Amazon y Microsoft en enero de 2024.
- "Más de treinta mil clínicos" = cifra de AP (2024), sobre la herramienta de Nabla basada en Whisper. No se afirma nada de cómo está Whisper hoy: en diciembre de 2023 ya había mejorado (12 de 187 seguían fallando).
- Hilke Schellmann es coautora del paper y del reportaje de AP; se cita a AP solo por el dato de Nabla.
- Sin logos de marcas (Google, Amazon, Microsoft, OpenAI): solo nombres en texto. El dibujo es esquemático.
